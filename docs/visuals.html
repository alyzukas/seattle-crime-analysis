Visualization Ideas

Group brainstorm: what each chart would show and how we plan to build it.

We will first identify the U.S. cities most similar to Seattle ("peer cities") based on population, income, poverty, cost of living, and health insurance coverage. We will then use these visualizations to compare Seattle with its peers, the nation, and the world.

  1. Is Seattle's crime higher or lower than its peer cities?National

    A ranked dot plot of violent crime per 100,000 residents for Seattle and its peer cities, with Seattle highlighted.

    Variables
    City, violent crime rate per 100k (most recent year)
    Encodings
    Position on x = crime rate; y = city, sorted by rate; color = Seattle vs. peers
    Design
    Grey dots for peers, one bright color for Seattle; direct labels instead of a legend

  2. Is Seattle following the same trend as its peers? How does it differ?National

    A multi-line chart of crime rates over time for each peer city (grey), with Seattle in color.

    Variables
    Year, city, crime rate per 100k
    Encodings
    x = year; y = rate; color = Seattle vs. peers
    Design
    Grey context lines; mark the 2021 reporting-system change

  3. Is the U.S. following the same trend as other countries? How does it differ?Global

    A multi-line chart of crime rates over time for each peer country (grey), with the U.S. in color.

    Variables
    Year, country, homicide rate per 100k (the most comparable measure across countries)
    Encodings
    x = year; y = rate; color = U.S. vs. other countries
    Design
    Grey context lines; label the U.S. line directly

  4. Do economic conditions relate to crime rates?National

    Faceted scatter plots comparing economic characteristics (median income, poverty rate, cost of living, population size, insurance coverage) against crime rates.

    Variables
    Census economic characteristics, violent and property crime rates per 100k
    Encodings
    x = economic measure (one facet each); y = crime rate; color = Seattle / peers / other cities
    Design
    Independent x-scales per facet; highlight Seattle and peers against grey cities

  5. Why are these cities Seattle's peers?National

    A heatmap of the similarity features (standardized as z-scores) for Seattle and its peer cities. This shows why they count as peers and supports our method in the final report.

    Variables
    City, each similarity feature (z-score)
    Encodings
    x = feature; y = city; color = z-score
    Design
    Diverging color scale centered at 0 (the national average)

  6. How does the U.S. compare with other countries?Global

    A heatmap of the similarity features (z-scores) for the U.S. and other countries.

    Variables
    Country, each comparison feature (z-score)
    Encodings
    x = feature; y = country; color = z-score
    Design
    Same diverging color scale as the city heatmap so the two can be compared

  7. How has Seattle's violent crime changed compared with other cities?Seattle

    A line graph of violent crime over time, with other cities in grey and Seattle in a bright color such as emerald.

    Variables
    Year, city, violent crime rate per 100k
    Encodings
    x = year; y = rate; color = Seattle vs. other cities
    Design
    Grey context lines; emerald for Seattle to make it stand out
Charts are interactive and need an internet connection to load.
